\subsection{Stopwords, queries, and optimization}
\subsection*{Summary: stopwords}

Token coverage uses NLTK English stopwords extended with corpus low-IDF terms (\texttt{corpus\_tfidf\_stopwords.csv} lists 250 corpus-specific candidates; the merged list has 448 types after union with NLTK). In a sample pass, 26.00\% of tokens matched NLTK-only; 27.12\% matched the TF--IDF low-IDF list only; after merging, 53.12\% of tokens were treated as stopwords (vocabulary size from the summary file: 214003.0 types).

\subsection*{Summary: queries}

The evaluation set contains 105 annotated queries in \texttt{data/processed/query\_templates.tsv} (exported to \texttt{queries.tsv} for retrieval). Intent types: informational (39), navigational (34), broad\_ambiguous (20), broad (10), transactional (2). Pooling and \texttt{qrels.tsv} judgments are described in the weekly sections; full tables remain under \texttt{outputs/eval/} if needed.

\subsection*{Summary: optimization}

The optimization step compared \textbf{disjunctive} vs \textbf{conjunctive} BM25 query evaluation. Mean batch latency was about 1929~ms (disjunctive) vs 1618~ms (conjunctive); documents scored were 105000 vs 92390. Overlap at rank 10 between the two rankings was approximately 0.520.
